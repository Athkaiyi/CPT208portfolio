SyncValley A Cozy Web App for Fairer Group Collaboration

SyncValley is a cozy, AI-supported group collaboration web app designed to reduce free-riding in student projects. Instead of building a separate game around teamwork, we redesigned the concept into a practical workflow tool that supports real group work through staged task planning, AI guidance, AI evaluation, visible contribution, coin rewards, and a gentle forest-growth feedback system.

02 · MOTIVATION

Why We Chose the Social Track

We chose the social track because unfair participation in student group projects is one of the most common and emotionally draining problems in university life. In many teams, a small number of students take responsibility for planning, coordination, and delivery, while others contribute less consistently or disengage until the final deadline. This creates frustration, weakens trust, and turns collaboration into a source of stress rather than shared learning.

Through reflection and feedback, we recognised that the issue is not only about monitoring contribution. Some students become passive because they do not receive timely positive reinforcement, while others avoid participation because they do not know how to begin or how to complete their assigned work. A meaningful solution therefore needs to support fairness, motivation, and task clarity at the same time.

SyncValley responds to this challenge by embedding support directly into the group workflow. It helps teams divide work into stages, limits each member to one task per stage to reduce task hoarding, uses AI to guide students through their work, evaluates submissions more consistently, and translates contribution into gentle visual growth through coins and a personal or shared forest. Rather than punishing low participation, it aims to make progress visible, manageable, and emotionally supportive.

03 · GAP DISCOVERY

What Existing Research and Products Still Miss

To identify design opportunities for SyncValley, we reviewed both academic work on group collaboration and existing digital productivity tools. Across both areas, we found strong support for planning, communication, and contribution tracking. However, we also found that many systems focus more on management than on the emotional and motivational realities of student teamwork.

This review helped us shift the project away from a fully game-like RPG prototype and toward a more embedded collaboration tool. We realised students do not need a separate game layered on top of project work. They need a system that fits naturally into their workflow while still offering encouragement, clarity, and fairness.

Combined Findings from Academic Papers & Commercial Products

Finding 1 — Coordination Exists, but Support Is Often Weak

  • Did well: many tools and frameworks support task division, milestone planning, and project structure.
  • Missed: they rarely help students who feel stuck, uncertain, or anxious about how to complete a task.

Finding 2 — Accountability Exists, but It Can Feel Punitive

  • Did well: existing systems often record participation, assign roles, or make progress visible.
  • Missed: contribution visibility is often stressful, surveillance-like, or overly competitive instead of supportive.

Finding 3 — Productivity Exists, but Gentle Motivation Is Missing

  • Did well: many products help teams stay organized and complete tasks efficiently.
  • Missed: few combine practical workflow support with timely positive feedback and emotionally safe motivation.
04 · PERSONAS

Primary and Secondary Users

We developed personas to represent two key user groups within student project teams. The first is the reliable contributor who often ends up carrying extra work and becomes frustrated when the team lacks fairness or visibility. The second is the hesitant participant who may be willing to help, but struggles with uncertainty, lack of confidence, or unclear expectations.

These personas helped us avoid designing only for already motivated students. SyncValley needed to support high-contributing members by making responsibility more transparent, while also supporting lower-confidence members with clearer starting points, AI guidance, and manageable task boundaries. This balance shaped the platform's stage logic, one-task-per-stage rule, and supportive feedback design.

05 · USER JOURNEY

From Unclear Group Work to Guided Contribution

Our journey mapping focused on the emotional and practical experience of a student moving through a group project. We looked at what happens when tasks are unclear, when one member contributes more than others, and when teams only realise their coordination problems too late.

The resulting journey highlighted several opportunities: students need clearer stage-based structure, smaller and more understandable tasks, support during execution, and a feedback system that makes contribution visible without creating shame. These insights directly informed the Home, Tasks, AI, and Garden flow in SyncValley.

06 · REQUIREMENTS

Playful Design Requirements

Based on our research, reflection, and user journey analysis, we redefined the design requirements to better match the current direction of SyncValley. The goal was no longer to build a full RPG experience, but to create a collaboration tool that remains gentle, motivating, and genuinely useful in real student workflows.

R1

Stage-Based Fair Coordination

The system must divide projects into clear stages, lock future stages until current work is finished, and limit each member to one task per stage. This helps prevent free-riding, reduces task hoarding, and makes responsibility more evenly distributed.

R2

AI Support for Starting and Finishing Work

The system must use AI to help students understand what to do, how to do it, and how to prepare evidence for submission. AI guidance should lower the barrier to participation, while AI evaluation should offer structured feedback and fairer reward allocation.

R3

Gentle Positive Feedback

The platform must make contribution visible in a low-pressure way. Instead of punitive warnings or stressful leaderboards, it should use coins, forest growth, and contribution snapshots to encourage participation while maintaining psychological safety.

08 · IDEATION
The Crazy Eights
Design Alternatives

Alternative 1 — Productivity Dashboard

This direction was practical for tracking deadlines and organizing tasks, but it felt too similar to existing project tools and did not respond strongly enough to the emotional and motivational problems we identified.

Alternative 2 — AI Chatbot Assistant

This direction supported task clarification and question answering well, but it focused too narrowly on individual help and was weaker in stage coordination, shared visibility, and contribution transparency across the team.

Alternative 3 — Cozy AI Collaboration Tool

This became the selected direction. It combines staged teamwork, one-task-per-member logic, AI guidance, AI evaluation, coin rewards, and a healing forest metaphor to support fairer and more sustainable participation.

09 · PROTOTYPE

Prototype and Core Flow

The prototype demonstrates the full logic of the revised SyncValley system. Users can move through Home, Tasks, AI, and Garden to understand the current project stage, accept one task per stage, receive AI guidance, submit evidence for AI evaluation, earn coins when their work passes evaluation, and use those coins to grow a personal forest while contributing to a team forest.

The core interaction loop is designed to support real group work rather than simulated play: AI helps teams plan stages, the system structures responsibilities, members receive support while working, evaluation updates contribution fairly, and visual growth provides gentle motivation. This keeps the experience functional while preserving the calm and encouraging tone of the product.

10 · EVALUATION & REFLECTION

Testing, Iteration, and Ethical Reflection

Through feedback and reflection, we discovered that our original pixel-style RPG concept was memorable but too detached from the realities of student collaboration. Users did not need a separate game around group work; they needed a more efficient, emotionally supportive system embedded in their actual project workflow. This became the major turning point of the project.

As a result, we iterated the concept into a cozy web app centered on stage coordination, AI support, and gentle progress feedback. We retained the warmth and motivational qualities of the original idea, but reduced the emphasis on role-play mechanics. This made the product more practical, more focused, and better aligned with the real needs we had identified: helping students start tasks, maintain motivation, and make contribution visible without pressure.

The project also raised important ethical questions. AI evaluation can make reward allocation feel more structured and consistent, but it should not become an unquestionable authority on human contribution. Likewise, contribution visibility must avoid public shaming or excessive surveillance. For this reason, SyncValley frames AI as a supportive assistant and uses soft, non-punitive visualisation such as forests and contribution snapshots rather than competitive rankings or warning systems.

What Worked

The project successfully identified two important participation barriers: low motivation and uncertainty about how to start. The revised concept addresses both through AI support, structured stages, and visible but gentle reward feedback.

What Needed Improvement

The original RPG framing was too strong and risked distracting from real workflow needs. We needed to simplify the concept, reduce unnecessary game framing, and make the system feel more immediately usable for student teams.

Iteration Focus

We shifted from a game-centred prototype to a workflow-centred collaboration tool, strengthened stage logic, clarified AI functions, and introduced the forest feedback system as a softer and more sustainable way to visualise contribution.
